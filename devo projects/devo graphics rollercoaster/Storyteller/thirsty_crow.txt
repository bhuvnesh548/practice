It was the middle of a very hot summer. The sun shone brightly in the sky, and the land had become dry and dusty.
A crow was flying across the countryside, searching for water.
He had been flying for many hours. His throat was dry, his wings were tired, and he was becoming weaker.
He searched near trees, rivers, and small ponds, but everywhere he looked, there was no water.
The crow began to worry.
"If I do not find water soon," he thought, "I may not have the strength to continue flying."
Just when he was about to give up, he noticed something shining near an old house.
The crow flew closer and discovered a large clay pot.
Excitedly, he landed beside it and looked inside.
There was water in the pot!
But there was a problem.
The water level was very low, and the crow's beak could not reach it.
He tried putting his head inside the pot, but it was too deep.
He tried pushing the pot, hoping to knock it over, but the pot was too heavy.
For a moment, the crow felt hopeless.
Then he noticed several small stones lying nearby.
The crow picked up one stone in his beak and dropped it into the pot.
Then he picked up another.
And another.
Slowly, the water level began to rise.
The crow realized what was happening and continued dropping stones into the pot.
He worked patiently, carrying one stone after another.
After some time, the water rose high enough for him to reach.
The crow happily drank the cool water.
His strength returned, and he spread his wings.
Before flying away, the crow looked back at the pot and thought, "A difficult problem can often be solved if we think carefully and refuse to give up."
Then he flew high into the blue sky.

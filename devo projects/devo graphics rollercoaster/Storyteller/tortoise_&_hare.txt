In a green forest lived a hare who was famous for being extremely fast.
The hare loved to show off his speed. Whenever he saw another animal, he would boast about how quickly he could run.
One of his favorite targets was a slow tortoise.
The hare would laugh whenever he saw the tortoise walking slowly along the forest path.
"Why do you move so slowly?" the hare asked one day. "By the time you reach the next tree, I could run around the entire forest!"
The tortoise looked at the hare calmly.
"Perhaps I am slow," he said, "but I never stop moving."
The hare laughed loudly.
"Never stop moving? That sounds like something slow animals say."
The tortoise became tired of being mocked.
"Then why don't we have a race?" he asked.
The other animals became silent.
The hare stared at the tortoise and then burst into laughter.
"A race? Between you and me? You must be joking!"
But the tortoise simply nodded.
The next morning, many animals gathered to watch the race.
The fox agreed to mark the starting point and the finish line.
"Ready!" shouted the fox.
"Go!"
The hare immediately raced forward like the wind.
Within seconds, he was far ahead of the tortoise.
The tortoise, meanwhile, continued walking slowly.
Step by step.
The hare looked back and could barely see the tortoise.
"This is too easy," he thought. "I have plenty of time."
He found a comfortable tree and decided to rest.
"I'll take a short nap," he said. "When I wake up, I can still finish before the tortoise reaches the halfway point."
Soon, the hare fell asleep.
Meanwhile, the tortoise continued walking.
He did not stop.
He did not complain.
He simply moved forward, one step at a time.
Hours passed.
Finally, the tortoise approached the finish line.
The animals began cheering.
Their noise woke the hare.
The hare opened his eyes and suddenly realized what had happened.
He jumped up and ran as fast as he could.
But it was too late.
The tortoise crossed the finish line first.
The forest erupted with cheers.
The hare lowered his head in embarrassment.
The tortoise smiled and said, "Speed is useful, but determination and consistency are often more important."
From that day on, the hare learned not to underestimate others.
the end.

Moral: Slow and steady wins the race.
